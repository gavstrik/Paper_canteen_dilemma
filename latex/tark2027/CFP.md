## Hard constraints
- **≤ 10 pages, excluding references.** "Extended abstracts".
- Proofs and other technical details may go in an **appendix**; reviewers are only expected to read the 10-page main text. → Main text must stand alone.
- **EPTCS LaTeX style** (https://style.eptcs.org/ → `eptcs.cls`, `eptcs.bst`). 11pt, 46 lines/page.
- **Not anonymised** — author names included.
- Max two submissions per author (incl. co-authorships).
- Submission via EasyChair: https://easychair.org/conferences/?conf=tark2027
- **AI-use disclosure is mandatory** at first submission: an extra paragraph (not counted toward the limit) describing any AI use (theories, proofs, code, data, *text*) and how AI-generated content was validated. AI cannot be an author. → Fed by `AI_LEDGER.md`.
- EPTCS: work must not be already published or under review elsewhere (confirmed by TSN 2026-11-09: not under review, not published; arXiv preprint exists).
- EPTCS requires DOIs in references where available.

## Review criteria
- Strong preference for topics of interest to an **interdisciplinary** audience.
- Main contribution must be **clearly identifiable**.
- Must explain **significance, novelty, implications**.
- Must **compare with and cite relevant literature**.

## Topics (relevant subset)
Semantic models for knowledge/belief/uncertainty · bounded rationality and resource-bounded reasoning · commonsense epistemic reasoning · epistemic logic · epistemic game theory · knowledge and action · formal analysis of games · foundations of multi-agent systems.

## Implication for this paper (Claude's read — not validated by authors)
Previous venues (Cognitive Science, PLOS ONE) were empirical/cog-sci. TARK reviewers are logicians, game theorists, philosophers, CS. The common-knowledge / coordinated-attack lineage (Halpern & Moses, Rubinstein's e-mail game, Fagin et al.) *is* TARK home turf — the formal analysis and the epistemic framing should become more prominent, not less.
